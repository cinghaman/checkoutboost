Delivery and pickup dates for florists and bakeries on Shopify: cutoffs, lead times and closed days

Published November 25, 2026 Smart Checkout Widgets All articles

A bouquet for Friday is worth nothing on Saturday. For a florist or a bakery the date is part of the product, and a Shopify checkout doesn't ask for it.

This guide sets up a calendar for that kind of shop with the delivery date picker in Smart Checkout Widgets. It works on every Shopify plan and is part of the app's paid plans.

Answer these first

  • How long does an order take to make? That is your lead time, and it may differ for pickup and delivery.
  • When do you stop taking orders for the next day?
  • Which days do you deliver, and which days can people collect?
  • Which dates are you closed or already full?

An example: a flower shop

Say a shop makes bouquets the day before, delivers Tuesday to Saturday and is open for pickup Monday to Saturday.

A customer ordering on Wednesday at 13:00 can have delivery on Friday. At 15:00 the cutoff has passed, so the first delivery day becomes Saturday. Pickup on Thursday is still open until 16:00. The times are the shop's, so a customer abroad sees the same days.

An example: a bakery

A bakery that sells bread and celebration cakes has a harder question, because bread can be ready tomorrow and a cake needs four days. The lead time applies to the whole store, so there are two ways to handle it:

  • Set the lead time for the slowest product. Simple, and bread customers wait longer than they need to.
  • Set it for the fastest product and say on the cake's page that it needs four days. You then check cake orders by hand.

Lead times per product are not available yet, so the first way is safer if cakes are most of what you sell.

Holidays and busy days

Add closed dates for public holidays and for days you can't take any more orders. A range works too, so one entry covers a week off. There is no automatic limit on orders per day, so close a busy day yourself when it is full.

For Valentine's Day or Mother's Day, close the days before and after if you only deliver on the day itself.

Make the date count

Switch on Require a date before checkout if an order without a date is useless to you. Checkout then also stops a customer who picks a delivery date and later chooses pickup. Leave it off if you take orders through draft orders or checkout links, since those are checked as well. Try your express buttons, such as Shop Pay, once after you switch it on.

The calendar doesn't change your shipping setup. Local delivery areas, pickup locations and their prices still come from Shopify. If some postcodes can't have delivery, a shipping rule by postcode handles that.

In the workshop

Each order shows its method and date under Additional details in Shopify admin. Add the Delivery date block to the Thank you and Order status pages too, so customers can check the day themselves.

Frequently asked questions

Can I set different cutoff times for delivery and pickup?

Yes. Lead days, the cutoff time and the days of the week are set separately for shipping, local delivery and pickup.

Can I set a lead time per product?

Not yet. The lead time applies to the whole store for each way to receive an order.

Can customers choose a time slot?

Not yet. They choose a day.

Can I limit how many orders I take per day?

There is no automatic limit. Add the day to your closed dates when it is full.

Which time zone is the cutoff in?

Your store's time zone, for every customer.
